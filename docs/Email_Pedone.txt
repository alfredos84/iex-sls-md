The LNDC results show that the network dilation anomaly is present both with and without Ca. Calcium seems to modify the magnitude of the LNDC slightly, but it does not drastically change the anomaly itself. Therefore, I would not make the LNDC difference the main message concerning the role of Ca. Rather, it seems that the basic mechanism responsible for the reduced dilation during IEX is preserved in both systems.

The Voronoi-volume results are consistent with this picture. In both Ca-containing and Ca-free glasses,

VV(Na, AM) < VV(K, IEX) < VV(K, AM),

showing that K introduced by ion exchange remains in a more confined local environment than K in the corresponding as-melted glass. This appears to be a robust characteristic of the ion-exchanged structure, essentially independent of the presence of Ca.

The coordination-number results are also interesting. In particular, the K coordination in the as-melted glasses decreases as K/(Na+K) increases. This is consistent with the idea that isolated K ions in a Na-rich modifier environment can have a denser oxygen coordination shell, whereas increasing K concentration progressively creates more open K-like environments. This seems consistent with the simultaneous increase in the K Voronoi volume.

I think the key missing structural analysis now is to separate the oxygen coordination of Na, K and Ca into bridging oxygens (BO) and non-bridging oxygens (NBO). At present we know that the total coordination number of K changes between AM and IEX glasses, but we do not know where the additional oxygen neighbors come from.

In particular, an important question is:

when K replaces Na and its coordination number increases, is this increase mainly due to additional NBOs or to BOs entering the K coordination shell?

This could provide a much more direct mechanistic interpretation of the IEX process. If the additional coordination mainly comes from NBOs, we should then determine whether these NBOs were initially associated with Na or Ca. This would tell us whether K competes with Ca for NBO environments or whether Ca remains essentially unaffected while K reorganizes the Na-associated modifier sites.

I would therefore compare, as a function of K/(Na+K):

CN(K–NBO) and CN(K–BO),

and similarly CN(Na–NBO/BO) and, for the Ca-containing system, CN(Ca–NBO/BO), for both AM and IEX glasses.

The other important missing piece is the atomic stress analysis. Since the structural changes are relatively subtle and the LNDC anomaly remains similar with and without Ca, atomic stresses may reveal more clearly how the presence of Ca affects the mechanical accommodation of the K/Na size mismatch.

It would be very interesting to compare the hydrostatic atomic stress distributions for K, Na, O, Si and Ca in the AM and IEX glasses, and then compare the K and O distributions between the Ca-containing and Ca-free systems. This could tell us whether Ca actually participates in carrying the stress generated by ion exchange or whether it mainly acts as a local constraint on the oxygen network.

At this stage, my interpretation is therefore that Ca does not fundamentally change the origin of the network dilation anomaly, but it may modify how the local oxygen environment reorganizes around the exchanged K ions and how the resulting elastic stress is distributed through the modifier network. The BO/NBO-resolved coordination and atomic-stress analyses may allow us to test this picture directly.

# === Per-atom stress (virial) ===
# 1=sxx, 2=syy, 3=szz, 4=sxy, 5=sxz, 6=syz
# stress*volume, with the LAMMPS stress/atom convention
compute vir all stress/atom NULL

# === Per-atom Voronoi volume ===
# Requires LAMMPS compiled with the VORONOI package (Voro++)
compute vor all voronoi/atom

# === Convert to stress (bar) and then GPa ===
variable vol      atom c_vor[1]
variable sxx_bar  atom -c_vir[1]/v_vol
variable syy_bar  atom -c_vir[2]/v_vol
variable szz_bar  atom -c_vir[3]/v_vol
variable sxy_bar  atom -c_vir[4]/v_vol
variable sxz_bar  atom -c_vir[5]/v_vol
variable syz_bar  atom -c_vir[6]/v_vol

# Conversion bar -> GPa
variable bar2gpa equal 1.0e-4

variable sxx_GPa atom v_bar2gpa*v_sxx_bar
variable syy_GPa atom v_bar2gpa*v_syy_bar
variable szz_GPa atom v_bar2gpa*v_szz_bar
variable sxy_GPa atom v_bar2gpa*v_sxy_bar
variable sxz_GPa atom v_bar2gpa*v_sxz_bar

# === Dump for OVITO ===
dump dOV all custom 1 initAL_dump_stress.atom id type x y z &
    v_sxx_GPa v_syy_GPa v_szz_GPa v_sxy_GPa v_sxz_GPa v_syz_GPa v_vol
